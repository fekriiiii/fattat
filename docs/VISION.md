# VISION.md

> The fixed point. Strategy and product hang off this. We pivot the layers below — we do not pivot this.
>
> Framework: Eric Ries, *The Lean Startup* — vision (the change in the world) → strategy (business model, customer, positioning; pivotable) → product (the expression; tuned constantly). Most teams build product first and never write the top. This doc fixes the top.

---

## The Vision

**Most students study hard and learn little — not because they're lazy, but because the way they study is broken. Fattat exists to make learning that actually works the path of least resistance.**

The enemy is re-reading, highlighting, cramming, and flashcard apps that *feel* productive and aren't. The bet the company is built on: **proven learning science — retrieval, spacing, interleaving, immediate feedback — shouldn't be a luxury that only disciplined or well-coached students get. It should be the default, baked so deep into the product that doing it the effective way is easier than doing it the lazy way.**

What this vision deliberately does **not** name: Högskoleprovet, ORD, LÄS, 399 SEK, Swedish. Those all live one layer down. If every one of them changed, the vision above would still be true. That is the test of a real vision — and it passes.

### Two forks, decided

These were the genuinely founder-level choices. Both are now locked:

1. **We are a pedagogy company, not an access company.** The enemy is *the broken way people study*, not *prep being locked behind money and coaching*. We win by making effective learning the default, not primarily by making existing prep cheaper. This is what the product's non-negotiable rules already prove — the vision just names it.

2. **Fattat is not, in its soul, an HP company.** The long-term ambition is to be the world's best learning app. HP is the first step — and a step that may last years — but it is a beachhead, not the ceiling. The vision is written broad on purpose. We do not pretend otherwise to ourselves, even while every near-term decision is 100% about winning HP.

## Strategy (hangs off the vision — and is pivotable)

This is the layer Ries says you adjust when reality pushes back. None of it is sacred.

- **Beachhead:** Swedish HP verbal prep (ORD + LÄS). *Why first:* a hard deadline plus a measurable outcome — your score moves or it doesn't — is the cleanest possible proving ground for "learning that works." If we can demonstrate the vision anywhere, it's here.
- **Customer:** HP candidates strong on kvantitativa where the verbal section drags the total down. The verbal-delta is the proof point and the marketing metric.
- **Wedge:** cognitive-science pedagogy as the *felt* difference in the product, not a tagline.
- **Model:** 399 SEK per provomgång (one-time, ~3 months access), 7-dagars gratis prov. Prove it on the founder first — private dogfooding toward HP **18 oktober 2026** — then build real marketing around an actual outcome (~late Nov '26), first monetization window våren 2027.

**What "pivotable" means here:** the beachhead could one day widen to other Swedish standardized tests, to language learning, or beyond Sweden entirely; the price could change; the customer could narrow or broaden. All of that can move *without betraying the vision*. That's the whole point of having written the vision down — it tells us which layer is allowed to move when something flops, so a healthy pivot doesn't get confused with aimless drift.

## Product (the vision compiled into code)

The non-negotiable rules are not UI preferences. They are the vision made physical — which is exactly why they're non-negotiable:

| Rule | What it expresses |
|---|---|
| No passive study mode | Learning that works is the *only* mode. |
| Question always before answer | Retrieval, not recognition. |
| No hint buttons, anywhere | The struggle to retrieve is where learning happens — don't short-circuit it. |
| FSRS decides word order | Spacing isn't optional, manual, or guessable. |
| Immediate feedback after every question | Close the loop while it still matters. |
| ORD before LÄS; enforced interleaving | The effective sequence is the default; the user doesn't get to opt into the lazy one. |

When someone — including future-you at 2am — asks "why can't we just add a hint button / a passive review mode / a skip?", the answer is on this page: because the vision says effective learning is the default, and that feature would make the lazy path easier than the effective one.
