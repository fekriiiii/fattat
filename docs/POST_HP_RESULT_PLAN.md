## The core trap this doc exists to prevent

The result is not binary good/bad, and the founder's own score is **n=1** — a great *story* but weak *proof*. One person's score moving could be talent, luck, or the method. The danger is landing in the flat/negative branch and pivoting the *vision* (abandoning the pedagogy bet) when the thing that actually needs adjusting is the *proof strategy* (get more data points) or the *messaging*.

---

## Branch A — Big verbal delta

**Trigger:** clear, large jump in verbal (ORD+LÄS) vs. the diagnostic Day-0 baseline; ideally a strong absolute verbal result too.

**What it means:** the dream case. Strongest possible proof of the pedagogy bet from the most credible angle (founder did the thing the app teaches and it worked).

**What we do:**
- Lead the launch with the delta as the headline proof.
- Build marketing v2 aggressively toward the **våren 2027 (10 april)** monetization window.
- Record/keep all the raw training footage and screenshots as the proof artifact.
- Still pursue a second/third data point (see "Proof depth" below) — one founder result, however good, is fragile alone.

## Branch B — Modest delta

**Trigger:** verbal improved, but not dramatically.

**What it means:** the method shows, but the personal-jump story isn't the hook. Common and totally survivable.

**What we do:**
- Reframe the message from "look at my huge jump" to **"look at how the method works"** — process and pedagogy as the lead, not the score.
- Lean harder on the cognitive-science differentiation that no Swedish competitor has.
- Proof depth (other students' results) becomes *more* important here, not less — it carries the weight the personal delta can't.

## Branch C — Flat or negative

**Trigger:** verbal didn't move, or moved down.

**What it means — and what it does NOT mean:** this does **not** mean the pedagogy bet is wrong. Likely explanations to rule out *before* concluding anything: founder was already strong on verbal (little room to move = ceiling effect), measurement noise on a single sitting, life/exam-day factors, or insufficient time-on-app during training. n=1 cannot distinguish "method doesn't work" from "this particular person had no room to improve."

**What we do (the branch nobody plans for, so here it is):**
- **Do not touch the vision.** A single flat personal result is not evidence against the pedagogy bet — it's the predictable risk of n=1.
- **Diagnose honestly first:** was there room to improve? Was the app actually used enough to be a fair test? Be brutally honest here rather than motivated-reasoning in either direction.
- **Shift the proof burden off the founder entirely** — this is the branch where other students' results aren't a nice-to-have, they're the whole game. The founder's result becomes a transparency/process story, not a proof story.
- **Consider the spring sitting (10 april 2027)** as a second personal attempt with lessons applied, rather than treating October as final.
- Slow the monetization timeline if needed rather than launching on a story you don't believe.

## Proof depth (cuts across all branches)

The founder's result is n=1 in every branch. The strength of the proof — and how much we can lean on it — depends on getting a second and third *motivated* student result.

- Sister is the obvious n=2 candidate but: (a) decoupled from October — any HP sitting works, incl. våren 2027; (b) must be a *genuinely motivated* user, not nagged — a dragged-along user is a contaminated experiment that proves nothing. Use her as a customer interview, not a sales target. (She's already an engaged tester — her "I won't do LÄS daily if I'm 17/20" drove the adaptive session balance feature.)
- Even n=2-via-sister is thin. The standing action across all branches: look for 2-3 other motivated HP students before launch.
