## The two levels (keep these separate — they demand different things)

| Level | What it is | Number (founder's pocket) | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The floor** | Get through university **without loans** | ~9–10k SEK/month net, *during study months* | A requirement. Must be solved. |
| **The dream** | Stable income to **marry in his early 20s** | ~20–30k SEK/month net, *stable and predictable* | The aim. Earned, not assumed. |

**Why "no loans" is a hard constraint, not a preference:** interest (ränta) is haram for the founder. CSN studiemedel is two parts — *bidrag* (a grant, no interest, fine to take) and *lån* (interest-bearing, off the table). The lån is the big part, ~9–10k/month. So the floor is precisely: **Fattat must replace what the CSN loan would have covered**, so the founder can decline the loan and live on bidrag + Fattat.

**Two truths about the numbers, stated plainly:**
1. **Revenue ≠ take-home.** Tax and costs come off the top. To *net* ~9–10k the floor needs more like **15k+/month in revenue**; the dream needs proportionally more.
2. **A sale is a lump sum, not monthly income.** If Fattat is ever sold, that's *capital* — the foundation the dream is built on, not the dream itself. The marriage-income still has to come from somewhere stable (the next project, reinvested capital, or a kept-and-grown Fattat).

## The central tension (this is the whole strategic problem)

HP prep, as priced today, produces **spiky seasonal revenue**: two sittings a year (spring ~10 april, autumn ~october), 399 SEK one-time, ~3 months access. Money arrives in two ~3-month bursts with little in between.

That is the *worst possible shape* for both levels:
- **The floor:** rent is monthly; income is twice-yearly. A normal student bridges a lean month with a loan — the founder can't. So he **must earn in the bursts and budget it flat across the year himself.** The plan has to build in that buffer discipline.
- **The dream:** a marriage-supporting income has to be *predictable* — something a partner and a household can be planned around. Spiky burst income can't carry that.

**Therefore the one strategic thread that pays off no matter what:** reduce the spikiness — build **year-round / recurring value**, not just the pre-exam pulse. This is the only lever that serves *both* the keep-path and the sell-path simultaneously, so it's a load-bearing line of the plan rather than a nice-to-have. (Subscriptions are still the wrong tool for a deadline product — people churn after the exam; see [MONETIZATION_RESEARCH.md](MONETIZATION_RESEARCH.md). The de-spiking comes from more cohorts, broader value, and eventually adjacent markets — not from forcing a monthly plan onto exam-takers.)

**The keystone:** everything downstream rests on the founder's own HP result in November 2026 — it's the proof the method works. His private training through summer/autumn 2026 is, literally, the most important business investment in this whole plan. No marketing trick beats "the founder raised his own verbal score — here's the evidence." Branch logic for each possible result lives in [POST_HP_RESULT_PLAN.md](POST_HP_RESULT_PLAN.md).

## The exit-agnostic spine (what we build, regardless of keep-or-sell)

The founder may keep Fattat or may sell it for a good offer — undecided, and rightly so. **There is no shortcut where you build something small and someone overpays.** A buyer pays for four things, and those four things are *also* exactly what makes a company worth keeping. So we build them either way:

1. **Predictable revenue** — the de-spiking thread above.
2. **Growth** — clear upward trend across the three windows.
3. **Defensibility (the moat)** — the cognitive-science pedagogy + the accumulating learning data (FSRS rating logs, prediction calibration, LÄS type profiling). Documented in [analysis/LEARNING_SCIENCE.md](../analysis/LEARNING_SCIENCE.md). No Swedish HP competitor has this.
4. **Market position** — be unambiguously **#1 in the niche.** "An HP app" is a small thing to sell to few buyers; "a proven learning engine + the leading brand in its category" is a much bigger thing to more (and richer) buyers. This is why VISION's broad framing isn't daydreaming — it raises the future sale price *and* the kept-business ceiling.

### Design constraint that serves both paths and the founder's life

**Minimize the founder's time-cost during university terms** — systems, automation, possibly outside help. This isn't a luxury:
- If **kept**, low-maintenance operation is what lets a full-time student run it.
- If **sold-later**, the founder has said he won't sell until the *next* project is already started — meaning there's an overlap period running Fattat *and* building the next thing. Low time-cost is what **buys the bandwidth** for that overlap.

## The option (last page, not the spine)

If a sale ever makes sense:
- **Legal structure matters.** You cleanly sell an **AB** (shares); an *enskild firma* is tied to the person and can only be sold as assets. The current entity is an enskild firma — the right call *now* (the founder is 16; an AB needs capital and is hard for a minor; see [LEGAL_SETUP.md](LEGAL_SETUP.md)). But **an AB conversion would need to happen before any sale.** On the radar, not on the calendar.
- **Timing rule (founder's own):** don't sell into a vacuum — no exit until the next project idea is already started and building. A sale gives capital *and* a soft landing, not just cash.
